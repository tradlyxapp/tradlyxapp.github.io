Privacy Policy

Effective November 7, 2026

This policy covers this website (tradlyxapp.github.io) and the Tradlyx apps: Tradlyx (stocks.tradlyx.app) and Household (household.tradlyx.app). They are private apps, used only by their owner and the members of one household, and are not offered to the public or meant for children.

Both run on the owner's own computer at home and are reached through Cloudflare, which carries the encrypted connection to it. They keep their data on that computer, show no ads, and never sell, rent or share it. The only outside services that receive anything are the ones named below, and each gets only what it needs to do its job.

This website

This website is a few static pages hosted by GitHub Pages. They set no cookies, run no analytics and have no forms. GitHub may keep its own record of visits, such as the address a request came from, under GitHub's privacy statement.

Tradlyx

Tradlyx has one user, its owner; nobody else can make an account.

What it keeps

  • The owner's account: the email address, the password (stored only as a hash), and, if two-factor sign-in is on, its secret (encrypted) and recovery codes (hashed).
  • Sign-ins and account activity: each signed-in device (browser, network address, when it was last used) and a log of sign-ins and sensitive changes with the same details, kept for a year, so the owner can spot and sign out anything unfamiliar. Never passwords or codes.
  • The brokerage connection: the owner's Robinhood sign-in token, encrypted, and what Tradlyx reads from that account: positions, orders, cash and account value. The owner's main account is read-only; orders are placed only in a separate account set up for automated trading, within the owner's limits.
  • Its own records: its calls and their reasons, trades, the practice portfolio, graded outcomes and lessons, notes, and the market data, news and filings it collected.
  • Backups: a copy of the database each night on the same computer, kept for 14 days.

Services it uses

  • Robinhood: the owner's brokerage. Tradlyx reads the account and, when the owner allows it, places orders there.
  • Anthropic (Claude): the AI model that does the analysis. It receives market data, news, filings, public posts from investing forums, and the owner's positions, cash and limits, only to analyse them. It runs on the owner's Claude subscription or, when that is busy, on Anthropic's API under the same terms.
  • Market data and news: Alpaca (prices, news and the practice account), Finnhub, Yahoo Finance, PR Newswire, the SEC's EDGAR, StockTwits, the economic calendars of the Federal Reserve (including FRED) and the Bureau of Economic Analysis, the House and Senate sites where members of Congress disclose their stock trades, public posts by public figures, and a short list of trusted news sites for the occasional web look-up. These receive only requests for public information, such as a stock symbol (and, for the SEC, the contact address it requires); nothing about the owner or the account.
  • Gmail: alert emails are sent from and to the owner's own Gmail account.

Text from news, filings and posts is treated as information, never as instructions.

Household

Household is used only by the members of one household, each with their own sign-in. It keeps the household's shared to-do list, groceries, shopping list, calendar, library, gym plans and timeline on the household's computer, and shows and controls only the home devices the household chose to share with it. Its Google sign-in (shown as "Household Reminders") can access a member's "Household" list in Google Tasks and, only if that member connects it, read-only access to their new Gmail messages to suggest tasks. Some of what members send (an email to the app, book titles, a photo of a book's cover) is processed by Anthropic's Claude, only to do what was asked.

Household has its own, detailed privacy policy, which lists everything it accesses and every service it uses, including how it handles data from Google APIs under the Google API Services User Data Policy and its Limited Use requirements. Where this summary and that policy differ, that policy applies to Household.

Uptime checks

An outside monitoring service (UptimeRobot) checks every few minutes that the apps are answering. It receives only a yes or no and the app's version, never anyone's information.
